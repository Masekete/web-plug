Every South African Business Needs a Website. Here Is Why Yours Does Not Have One Yet.

Webify Team
June 27, 2026
5 min read

There is a conversation that happens in township hair salons, at spaza shop counters, in backyard mechanic bays, and at market stalls all over South Africa. Someone asks: "Do you have a website?" And the answer is almost always: "No, we just use Facebook" or "We can't afford that."

Both of those answers are understandable. But both of them are quietly costing those businesses customers every single day.

This post is not going to lecture you about the future of digital business. It is going to be honest with you about why a website matters for your specific situation in South Africa, why the pricing in this industry has historically locked out the people who need it most, and what a real solution looks like.

South Africans Are Online. Your Customers Are Looking for You.

South Africa has over 45 million active internet users. The average South African spends more than nine hours online every day, mostly on a mobile phone. People in Tembisa, in Mitchells Plain, in Polokwane, in Butterworth are using Google to find plumbers, hair salons, caterers, mechanics, tutors, and every other kind of service you can think of.

When they search, Google shows them results. Those results include businesses with websites. If your business does not have a website, you are not in that conversation at all.

Facebook helps, and we will come back to that. But Facebook alone is not enough, and here is the honest reason why.

Why Facebook Is Not a Substitute for a Website

Facebook is useful. It lets you post updates, connect with customers, and run ads. But it has three fundamental problems that a website does not.

First, you do not own it. Facebook can restrict your page, reduce who sees your posts, or change its rules at any time. Businesses have had their pages removed overnight with no warning and no way to get them back. When that happens, every follower you built over years disappears with it. A website is yours. Nobody can take it from you.

Second, Facebook does not rank well on Google. When someone types "catering services in Durban" or "affordable electrician Pretoria" into Google, a Facebook page rarely appears near the top. A properly built website does. That difference in visibility translates directly into customers finding you or not finding you.

Third, a Facebook page does not look as professional as a website when someone is making a buying decision. Think about the last time you considered hiring someone for a significant job. You probably looked them up. A business with a website that clearly explains what they do, shows their work, and has a way to contact them instantly feels more trustworthy than one with just a Facebook page. That trust is what converts interest into a phone call.

💡

Think of it this way: Facebook is the street outside your shop where people discover you. Your website is the actual shop where they decide whether to buy. You need both, but you cannot replace the shop with the street.

The Real Reason Most Township and Small Businesses Do Not Have Websites

It is not laziness. It is not that business owners do not understand the internet. It is pricing.

Walk into most web design agencies in South Africa and ask for a basic business website. You will hear numbers starting at R5,000, climbing quickly to R15,000 or R20,000, and sometimes much higher. For a large business with a healthy marketing budget, that is a normal business expense. For a hair salon in Mamelodi, a catering business in Khayelitsha, or a construction company just getting started in Limpopo, that number is not just expensive. It is impossible.

So people settle for Facebook. Not because it is better. Because it is free and the alternative feels out of reach.

This is the gap that exists in the South African market and it is the gap that leaves thousands of genuinely good, hardworking small businesses invisible online while their more resourced competitors take the customers that should rightfully be shared across the industry.

What a Website Actually Does for a Small Business

A website works for your business every hour of the day, including when you are asleep, on a job, or on holiday. Here is what that means in practice.

Someone in your area searches for the service you offer at 10pm on a Sunday night. They find your website. They read about what you do, see photos of your work, check your prices, and send you a WhatsApp enquiry. By the time you wake up on Monday morning, there is already a potential customer waiting for your response. That is a sale your business would have missed entirely without a website.

A website also gives your business something that no social media page can fully replicate: a professional address on the internet that belongs to you. An email address like yourname@yourbusiness.co.za instead of yourname@gmail.com. A place where everything about your business lives in one organised space. That kind of digital presence builds trust with customers in a way that is hard to put a number on but very easy to feel.

🌍

The shift is already happening: South Africa's online market grew by 38% in 2025 while physical retail grew by under 3%. Customers are moving online faster than most businesses are moving with them. The businesses with a strong web presence now are the ones capturing that growth.

What You Actually Need (And What You Do Not)

A lot of the cost in web design comes from things that most small businesses simply do not need. Complex e-commerce systems. Custom animations. Ongoing monthly content management contracts. Database integrations. These are features built for large businesses with technical teams, and small business owners often end up paying for them because agencies bundle everything together.

What a township or small business in South Africa actually needs from a website is straightforward:

  • A clean, professional design that works properly on a phone
  • A clear explanation of what you do and who you do it for
  • Photos of your work or your products
  • Your contact details, WhatsApp number, and location
  • A way for Google to find you through basic search engine optimisation (SEO)

That is it. A website that does those five things well will generate real enquiries for your business. It does not need to be complicated. It needs to be done properly.
